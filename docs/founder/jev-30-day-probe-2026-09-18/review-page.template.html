How the probe works

Jev doesn't write answers. It reads a fixed state and returns a probability for each question you define. Your profile goes in every request, alongside one action at a time.

State

Your profile, your constraints, and one action

Questions

10 questions, each answered on its own: 8 yes/no probabilities and one 0–3 score

Repeat

Every action runs 3 times, so we can see how much the numbers wobble

Rank

Code combines the answers using weights you can change later without asking Jev again

Before you trust any number: what Jev is and isn't built for

TypeSafe's documentation for yes/no questions says to avoid questions that require world knowledge or predictions about future events. Jev's calibration claim covers judgments about evidence in the state you give it. Seven of these ten questions are forecasts, including "will you reach a million?" For those, a 0.12 is Jev's structured opinion, not a measured 12% chance.

That's why the list includes controls. Six anchors have obvious answers (lottery tickets, meme coins, selling your gear). If Jev gets them wrong, its forecasts are noise. Three twins restate another action in different words, and their scores should match. Three repeats show how much each number moves. The three Evidence questions are the ones Jev is designed for.
