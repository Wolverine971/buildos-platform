# Project-accountability and feedback communities: what works, what kills, what they charge

Research date: 2026-10-17. Web research only; no code changes. Claims that rest on a single third-party page, on a vendor's own survey, or on undated material are marked **UNVERIFIED**. Where sources conflict, both figures are shown.

Framing for the BuildOS question: the founder is weighing "join a group of people working on their projects" as the front door, with BuildOS as the engine that drives check-ins and feedback. The ads cohort (81 signups) was focus/follow-through people; 73 wanted a community and 69 wanted weekly calls. This report is a stress test of that idea against what has actually happened to comparable communities.

## 2. What retains, what kills

### 2a. What retains (with the best available evidence)

**Showing up is the behaviour; everything else is downstream.**

- Ruzuku platform data (26,632 courses, 1,253,716 enrollments, 2011-Aug 2026, observational): completion 53.4% for scheduled cohorts vs 42.9% drip vs 41.9% self-paced. Students active in week one complete at 78.3% vs 17.2% for those who are not. Courses with lesson discussions complete at 50.9% vs 37.3%. **Cohorts of 11-25 complete highest (65.2%); 1-10 is 51.5%; 100+ is 51.3%.** Self-selection acknowledged. https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/course-success-index
- Maven's "~75% completion" is self-reported platform data, repeated by a16z; the "90%+" figure traces back to Maven's own claims, not an independent study. https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-maven/ ; https://aienablement.academy/insights/cohort-completion-rates-honest
- Skool churn guidance (vendor, not measured on Skool): most churn happens in the first 30 days among members who never posted, attended, or completed anything; "a recurring call creates attendance, a recorded library generally does not." https://www.skoolfiles.com/blog/average-skool-churn-rate

**Weekly progress reports to a specific person is the best-supported accountability mechanic, and the effect is smaller than the blogs say.**

- Gail Matthews (Dominican University): 267 recruited, 149 completed, randomised into 5 groups over 4 weeks; mean self-rated goal achievement: Group 1 unwritten 4.28; Group 2 written 6.08; Group 3 written + action commitments 5.08; Group 4 + sent to a friend 6.41; **Group 5 + weekly progress reports to a friend 7.6** (significantly higher than all others). Note: self-rated scale, 44% attrition, 4 weeks. The widely quoted "76% vs 43%" is not in the summary. https://www.dominican.edu/sites/default/files/2020-02/gailmatthews-harvard-goals-researchsummary.pdf
- The "ASTD: 65% if you commit to someone, 95% with an accountability appointment" statistic has **no locatable primary source**; secondary sources contradict each other on what the 65% even means. Treat as apocryphal. **UNVERIFIED.**
- stickK contracts (KAIST analysis of 1,000 posts): success 74.2% with a friend as referee vs 68.7% self-referee (p<.0005). Adding social _and_ financial devices together may reduce the effect. https://ic.kaist.ac.kr/publications/papers/lee2019commitment.pdf

**Small, same-stage groups with a named facilitator.**

- Hampton: core groups of 5-8, curated by locale/industry/stage/model, run by paid executive facilitators, ~10 two-hour meetings a year, member rating <8 triggers a follow-up; revenue minimum raised from $1M to $3M to keep stages matched. https://community.inc/article/hampton
- Never Search Alone: councils of 4-6 split by tenure and by "unemployed fast-seekers / working while searching / students"; volunteer moderator who is a peer. https://www.neversearchalone.org/faq
- Flow Club cut its session cap from 9 to 5 because "the 9-person limit was too high" once 30-minute sessions proliferated; small groups chosen over 1:1 because absence does not break the session. https://www.flow.club/blog/body-doubling-session-format
- Ultraspeaking: breakout rooms of 3, 3-4 reps per class, feedback on every rep. https://ultraspeaking.com/professional-l1/
- Mastermind practitioners converge on 4-8, meeting every other week; "weekly is hard to sustain past the first few months unless the group is professional." No controlled study on cadence exists. https://www.thesuccessalliance.com/blog/how-many-members-should-be-in-a-mastermind-group/ ; https://mastermindbetter.com/groups/how-to-run-a-mastermind-group/
- Duolingo (ex-CPO Jorge Mazal): a friends-based leaderboard "wasn't particularly effective"; matching users against others _at a similar engagement level_ raised overall learning time 17% and tripled highly engaged learners. Engagement-match beat relationship-match. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth

**A fixed ritual on the calendar beats a space to hang out in.**

- Vassallo: the $5/mo Circle community of 1,000 "died within weeks"; Small Bets runs on a rolling event calendar because "calendar notifications keep people engaged." https://community.inc/article/small-bets
- Writers' Hour: 9 -> 30 -> 93 -> 800+/day on a single invariant (silent hour, 8am in four time zones). https://signup.writershour.com/
- Shut Up & Write: 100,000+ members on "no one will read or critique your writing." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shut_Up_%26_Write!

**Paid beats free for retention; annual beats monthly; curated beats open.**

- Paid community churn benchmarks (vendor compilations, not audited): 4-9%/month typical; 2-3%/month for curated communities above $200/mo with active programming; 5.8%/month "average" (retentioncheck 2026); annual plans 92% 12-month retention vs 68% monthly (Baremetrics via Kourses, **UNVERIFIED**). Top cancellation reasons in one survey: low engagement 32%, value didn't justify cost 27%. https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/membership-communities ; https://kourses.com/member-retention/ ; https://communipass.com/blog/how-to-reduce-churn-in-a-paid-community-12-retention-strategies-that-actually-work-in-2026/
- Jacobson (Focusmate): waited too long to charge; "paying users give higher-quality feedback"; set $5/mo deliberately low "because it would be very inclusive." https://mixergy.com/interviews/focusmate-with-taylor-jacobson/
- Parr: "charge more money than you think"; $300 Trends membership "couldn't afford to provide certain services."

**Feedback protocols that make feedback safe and specific.**

- Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process: (1) statements of meaning, (2) the maker asks questions, (3) responders ask neutral questions, (4) opinions only with the maker's consent ("I have an opinion about X, would you like to hear it?"). The maker controls scope. https://lizlerman.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Critical-Response-Process-in-Brief_CRP-one-pager_updated-2020_03_24.pdf
- Critique Circle/Scribophile price reciprocity: effort-weighted credits, zero credit under 150 words, a "spotlight" that guarantees reads. https://www.critiquecircle.com/faq/10/

### 2b. What kills

**Scale without structure.**

- Buildspace S4: 18,000 participants, ops spread over 7 tools, "the team missed demos", "volume of projects became hard to track"; strong builders got no traffic when Twitter's algorithm changed. Closed 3 months after its biggest cohort. https://www.therunway.ventures/p/buildspace
- Parr: "A community... if it gets too big and too fast, it sucks."
- Vassallo's Circle community: grew to 1,000, "activity died within weeks."

**Founder dependence and host burnout.**

- Buildspace: solo founder; "the desire to push like hell isn't burning as it once did"; secondary accounts of panic attacks and depression.
- Write of Passage: NPS 75 and a "radiant" community, still closed because the founder could not both run the company and write, and the bi-annual cohort model never produced "a dependable flow of new students."
- Flow Club's founder hosted 25 sessions a week before members took over hosting. The fix that worked was turning members into hosts with a 50% discount, not hiring. https://www.flow.club/blog/body-doubling-session-format ; https://in.flow.club/plans/
- Dynamite Circle runs a full-time community manager who moderates, organises global events, and matches masterminds. https://tropicalmba.com/episodes/ten-years-dynamite-circle/

**Bad actors and moderation load.**

- DC founders: "making sure they don't contaminate the group is typically the point of failure"; "it can only take a handful of those people to wreck the whole thing"; removing bad actors matters more than generating discussion ("via negativa").
- NaNoWriMo: forum moderation scandal (2023-24) -> forums read-only, never reopened -> loss of the community's main organ a year before closure.
- Writing-group practitioners: a single toxic member often ends a group; groups without ground rules and a moderator go sour; friendly groups stop giving hard feedback. https://livinghappy.substack.com/p/ways-writing-critique-groups-can ; https://annerallen.com/2011/10/bad-critique-groups8-things-that-can/

**Free riders and reciprocity collapse.**

- Nielsen's 90-9-1 (2006) is a heuristic, not a law; in chat communities 10-25% may post monthly (**UNVERIFIED**). https://churbuck.com/2006/10/31/
- Product Hunt: vote swaps "degrade the system"; the platform runs vote-ring detection and penalises incentivised upvotes. The same decay hits "feedback for feedback" if feedback is unpriced. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9930582
- Discord -> Skool migrations: anecdotes of 3,000 -> ~1,500 and 800 -> 300 members when a free chat space became a paid structured one ("the rest wanted to hang out and chat, not pay"). https://stickyhive.ai/skool/migrate-from-discord/

**Mismatched stages.**

- Hampton raised its floor from $1M to $3M revenue; it first tried marketers and "not many of them wanted it"; Parr did "50 to 70 meetings... none of them wanted it" before fit. https://x.com/thesamparr/status/1829138094064390203
- On Deck: too many fellowships, "fractured" focus and brand, two layoff rounds in 2022.
- Never Search Alone explicitly separates fast-seekers from employed seekers from students.

**"Nobody wants to read my thing."**

- Critique groups read a few pages at a time, so feedback drifts to line edits ("end-stage stuff") and structural feedback never happens. https://www.writebynight.net/abcs-of-writing/strategies/critique-group-flaws/
- Purpose mismatch (critique group vs accountability group vs drafting group) breeds resentment. https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22411382-why-your-amazing-writing-group-might-be-failing-you
- Credit economies exist precisely because unpaid reading does not happen at scale.

**Confidentiality.**

- YPO forums: "Nothing, Nobody, Never"; even spouses excluded; conflicts of interest disclosed up front. https://www.ypo.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/YPO-Code-of-Conduct-FY18-19.pdf
- Hampton: all members sign a confidentiality pledge. Never Search Alone: confidentiality is a ground rule.
- Implication: client work and stealth projects need a _closed small group with a signed norm_, not a public feed. Public build-in-public formats (WIP, Ship 30, Buildspace) only work for people whose projects can be public.

**Platform dependence.**

- Slack moved free workspaces to a 90-day history cap on Sept 1, 2022 (older messages hidden, later deleted). https://www.forbes.com/sites/barrycollins/2022/07/28/slack-imposes-90-day-cut-off-on-free-accounts/
- Buildspace's seven-tool stack "with no tight control over experience quality."
- NaNoWriMo lost its forums, the thing members actually used, to a moderation failure.
- Discord's real-time chat is what members miss when moved to forum-style platforms; hybrid setups (Discord for voice coworking, Skool/Circle for structure) are common. https://stickyhive.ai/skool/vs-discord/

**Economics.**

- Perell: "built something worth celebrating in every way, except for the economics." Cohort cadence = lumpy demand.
- Buildspace: free product, sponsorship revenue "capped at about $2-3M per year", alternatives "conflicted with the mission of free access."
- DC: "very difficult to run, not that profitable"; advice is to pair community with a product or service.
- Small Bets ads: $2,190 -> $3,184 revenue (gross), "mixed results."

**AI-era specific.**

- NaNoWriMo's Aug/Sept 2024 AI statement cost it board members and a sponsor within days; the organisation later apologised and dropped the language. For a community of makers, "what counts as your work" must be explicit before the first dispute. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month
- Colin & Samir's Discord thumbnail-critique channel failed because shared images did not look like YouTube; the fix was a bot that previews title + thumbnail in situ. Feedback needs the artefact in its real context. https://www.drewlyton.com/story/i-made-a-discord-bot-for-testing-youtube-thumbnails-post/
- The "AI accountability coach" category (Liftora, Coach Call AI, Nag Bot, Commit, NOZERO, Best Version) launched 2025-26 with no published retention. The human-vs-AI body double VR study found human doubles give stronger accountability but can feel evaluative/stressful; AI doubles were "safe accountability." https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153

## 3. ADHD body doubling and accountability: the evidence

**Status of the science (2023-2026): survey-based and small lab studies; no RCT.**

- Eagle, Baltaxe-Admony, Ringland, ASSETS 2023 ("Proposing Body Doubling as a Continuum of Space/Time and Mutuality") and ACM TACCESS 2024 ("It Was Something I Naturally Found Worked and Heard About Later"): survey of 220 people (193 neurodivergent, ~75% actively use body doubling, per a secondary source, **UNVERIFIED**). Two axes: same vs different space/time; ambient companionship vs performance/accountability. Many "recognise it as a strategy they have engaged in for years." https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3597638.3614486 ; https://leyabreanna.com/papers/body_double_taccess.pdf
- EEG study (ACM, 2025, n=26, NT + medicated + unmedicated ADHD): **no statistically significant difference** between body-doubling and solo conditions; no negative effects; trends favourable for unmedicated ADHD. https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3663547.3759743
- VR study (arXiv Sept 2025, n=12 adults with ADHD, bricklaying task): faster completion and higher perceived attention with a human or AI double vs alone; authors state "no peer-reviewed controlled trials have examined its impact on adults with ADHD." https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153
- Mixed-reality roadmap (arXiv 2026): research "mainly very recent and limited in number." https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07851
- ADHD coaching for adults: reviews count only 2-3 RCTs among ~18 outcome studies; a 2024-25 RCT of Work-MAP (telehealth metacognitive program, n=46, 11 weekly sessions) showed gains in work performance and executive function at 3-month follow-up. https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1182373.pdf ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11859935/

**Vendor data (treat as marketing).**

- Focusmate Feb 2024 survey: n=834 members who had completed 5+ sessions in the prior week (survivorship built in); 99% say it improves productivity; self-estimated +143% average; neurodivergent segment +161%; diagnosed ADHD n=252, 100% report improvement. No retention, no-show, or completion rates reported. https://www.focusmate.com/virtual-coworking-research/
- Jacobson: "so many people in our community are neurodivergent and have ADHD"; the product works partly through "the sense of being accepted." Earlier press: ~28% of users have an ADHD diagnosis, more than a third identify as neurodivergent. https://www.fastcompany.com/90632591/focusmate-body-doubling-virtual-coworking

**What ADHD organisations and coaches say works / fails** (r/ADHD could not be crawled; ADDA, CHADD, ADDitude used instead).

- Both ADDA and CHADD state there is no research proving effectiveness. https://add.org/the-body-double/ ; https://chadd.org/adhd-news/adhd-news-adults/adhd-weekly-could-a-body-double-help-you-increase-your-productivity/
- Failure modes named repeatedly: sessions drift into socialising when the double is a friend; vague setup ("most body doubling fails because it's too vague"); a double who supervises or talks costs energy and becomes a distraction; body doubling solves _starting_, not _deciding what to do_; novelty fades; for some AuDHD adults the cost of processing another person exceeds the initiation benefit; a human double can feel evaluative or stressful for anxious people. https://www.adhdcareconnect.com/resources/adhd-body-doubling-accountability-strategies ; https://www.builtforadhd.com/articles/what-is-body-doubling-adhd
- Distinction drawn by coaches: body doubling = presence at the moment of starting (minutes); accountability partner = check-ins across weeks. Different jobs, different cadences.
- ADDA's own format: Pomodoro-style "Productivity PowerHour+" groups; coach clients use 50-minute blocks and break a project into what fits in one. https://add.org/adda-virtual-peer-support-group-studyhall-pomodoro-style/
- Free ADHD communities on Skool (ADHD Nation 1.1k, ADHDo it Club 325 with 24h weekday sprints and a weekly planning call) show the market's shape: free body doubling as the hook, coaching programs ($1,498 at ADHD Harmony) as the product.

**Implication for BuildOS:** the ADHD-adjacent "focus and follow-through" segment responds to _presence at the moment of starting_ (daily, short, low-stakes) and to _a specific person expecting a report_ (weekly). Neither is "feedback on my project." The 69-of-81 "weekly calls" answer is consistent with the weekly-report mechanic; the daily piece is better served by sessions than by calls.

- Ruzuku: completion is a retention proxy; first-week activity is the single biggest split (78.3% vs 17.2%).

**Does "join a community of people building things" work as ad creative?**

- No public ad-economics data from Skool, Hormozi, LWS, or Flow Club. Hormozi's Skool investment amount is undisclosed; "$100M" is a third-party headline. **What the tool must do for a project-accountability community to work (synthesis):**

1. Make showing up near-zero friction and never wasted: match on task/stage/engagement (Focusmate, Duolingo), keep groups small enough that absence does not break the session (Flow Club 5), rematch when someone flakes.
2. Make the unit of progress legible and hard to fake: "what moved" has to be generated from real work state, not typed in. GitHub removed streaks partly because they were fakeable; WIP only counts shipped items.
3. Put the ritual on the calendar and remind: Vassallo's events calendar; Hampton's quarterly group launches and ~10 meetings/yr; Writers' Hour at 8am x4 time zones.
4. Score reliability, not output: Focusmate's attendance score is the quiet engine; Skool had to add churn to stop rewarding spikes.
5. Keep the artefact in context when asking for feedback (thumbnail bot), and let the maker set the questions (CRP step 2).
6. Price reciprocity if feedback is part of the promise (credits), or do not promise feedback at all (Shut Up & Write).
7. Do not let the tool become the moral authority: streak shame (WIP, GitHub) and MRR leaderboards (Skool) produced the behaviours their communities later had to undo.
