# LinkedIn — 18 projects, one shared context

**Hook (first line, stands alone):**

> I have 18 projects open right now. My problem has never been ideas.

**Body:**

Two products. A handful of client sites. A precision-shooting business. A training program I'm running on myself. Even a dumb music project I refuse to kill.

For years the thing that broke wasn't ambition — it was the switch. Every time I moved from one project to the next, I'd lose the thread. The first 20 minutes of any work block went to remembering where I left off: what I'd decided, what was half-finished, what the actual next step was. Multiply that by a dozen switches a day and that's the whole day gone.

So I built the thing I needed. Now every project keeps its own living context — the tasks, the decisions, the loose ends — so when I drop back in, it's already there. I don't rebuild the mental model. I just keep going.

The bottleneck was never having too many ideas. It was **holding the context between them.**

**Close:**

That's what BuildOS is: the place that remembers so you don't have to. If you're running more than one thing at a time — what breaks first for you: focus, follow-through, or context?
