# BuildOS past-signup email strategy — September 10, 2026

Proposed campaign and reviewable copy. This document does not change stored drafts, enroll recipients, or send email.

**The offer: bring one current project, get a useful next step, then come back and update the same project.** That gives a past signup something concrete to try and a reason to return. The campaign should demonstrate that loop before explaining the wider product.

This recommendation draws on the four supplied source analyses, the existing welcome sequence, the general retargeting campaign, and the July beta framework and representative v2 drafts. The July README records 86 original beta signups and 83 saved drafts. Those are historical campaign figures, not a verified current list size or send history. Some older documents disagree about segment totals. Reconcile current eligibility and past sends before selecting a batch; do not treat the July notes as proof that nobody has since been contacted.

These are directional lessons. Their cold-email reply-rate figures and newsletter revenue anecdotes are not forecasts for BuildOS.

**What to keep and change in the existing content**

The [welcome sequence](../../strategy/buildos-welcome-sequence.md) already contains the strongest material: a rough-input prompt in Email 2 and a second-session exercise in Email 3. Reuse those ideas in shorter copy. They teach a behavior the recipient can try immediately.

The [general retargeting copy](../retargeting/EMAIL_COPY.md) spends considerable space explaining accumulated context and collaboration. For a dormant individual, that asks for too much conceptual agreement before giving a useful action. Replace the teaching arc with: current problem → small project test → update that project later. Save collaboration for someone who specifically asked for it.

The [July beta framework](FRAMEWORK.md) correctly distinguishes people who left work behind, account holders who never got started, and people who only requested access. Preserve that distinction, attributed signup context, and the option of founder help. In the [v2 drafts](DRAFTS_v2.md), tighten these recurring issues:

- Choose either a product link or a reply request as the main CTA. Several examples ask readers to do both.
- Remove invented recollection such as “that line stuck with me” unless DJ actually remembers it that way. “When you signed up, you wrote…” is sufficient.
- Describe known facts; do not assert why someone left or why the founder was silent unless verified.
- Replace absolute readiness claims and “nothing to learn” with a bounded, honest invitation to try one workflow.
- Use neutral subject lines. Sensitive signup disclosures and revealing project titles do not belong in inbox previews. When relevant, use a non-sensitive signup detail in the body with attribution; do not mine private project contents for marketing.
- Refresh dates, account existence, project availability, and prior contact history. A saved draft is not a current recipient assessment.

**Choose the invitation from what happened before**

| Recipient group                                                                | Message                                                                                                              | One main CTA                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Requested beta access; never created an account                                | “You signed up for the BuildOS beta. Here is a small way to try it now.”                                             | Start a first project through registration.          |
| Has an account; never created usable work                                      | “You made an account a while back. Bring one project you are working on now.”                                        | Open BuildOS and supply the rough input.             |
| Created work; now dormant                                                      | “Pick up one project and add what changed.” Verify that the old project is still accessible before promising it.     | Resume that project; otherwise choose a current one. |
| A known relationship or substantive signup answer; likely to benefit from help | Offer a short plan from information they choose to send now. This can apply to account holders or waitlist contacts. | Reply with the project and blocker.                  |
| Known unresolved product problem                                               | A personal note only when the relevant fix is confirmed. Acknowledge the specific issue accurately.                  | Try that repaired workflow or reply for help.        |

Exclude current active users from win-back messaging. Honor unsubscribe, complaint, hard-bounce, manual-stop, and reply records; remove internal/test accounts. Use verified recipient addresses. Existing guessed address corrections require evidence before reuse. Check the original signup/email permissions rather than automatically enrolling everyone in a newsletter.

Relationship stage should determine the CTA; declared use case should determine the example. Do not create dozens of persona campaigns for a small list. If a contact named a book, use a book example; if they named a launch, use a launch example; if little is known, use the general version.

**Run two invitations, then follow behavior**

| Timing                            | Who gets it                                                                                         | Purpose                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Day 0                             | Eligible recipients in the current small wave                                                       | Re-establish context and offer one useful project start.                                        |
| Day 4–5                           | No reply, no return, still eligible                                                                 | Give a concrete input prompt that removes the work of figuring out what to say.                 |
| Day 10–12, optional               | A manually reviewed, identifiable return without a meaningful action, or other clear human interest | Help with the specific obstacle. This is not a blanket third email to nonresponders.            |
| After the first meaningful action | Someone who saved useful project work but has not returned to update it                             | Invite the second session. Use the relevant welcome/lifecycle message where one already exists. |
| After two useful sessions         | Recipients whose email preferences permit it                                                        | Offer an optional daily brief or an occasional practical email matched to their work.           |

Any reply ends the scheduled sequence and moves the person to a human conversation. Any return ends the generic “come back” messages: a visitor who got stuck needs assistance; someone who created useful work needs the second-session prompt. Stop proactive win-back outreach after two silent touches. Do not reset the sequence under another campaign name.

Use roughly 24–48 hours after the first action for the second-session reminder, adjusted around other email already sent. Keep proactive promotional messages at least 72 hours apart and at most two in a rolling seven days across campaigns. Personal replies can happen promptly. These timing choices are starting assumptions, not proven optimal intervals.

Do not use “opened” alone to authorize an extra touch. Apple explains that Mail Privacy Protection can download remote content in the background regardless of whether the recipient engages. Use replies and actual product behavior; corroborate clicks with an identifiable visit where possible. [Apple's explanation](https://www.apple.com/uk/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/)

**Drafts to review**

Use `DJ at BuildOS` and a monitored reply address. These are templates, not recipient-ready claims. Resolve account state and links before use. The sending system should append the appropriate sender identification and a visible unsubscribe link; the unsubscribe is separate from the conversion CTA.

**1. First invitation — someone who only signed up for beta access**

Subject: `You signed up for BuildOS`  
Preview: `Bring one unfinished project. Start with the rough version.`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> You signed up for the BuildOS beta a while back. I'm DJ, the founder.
>
> Here's a small way to try it: bring one project you're working on now. Tell BuildOS what you're trying to do, what's already happened, and what's getting in the way. It can help turn that into a project with next steps you can review and change.
>
> Start with this: “I'm trying to **_. Right now _**. I'm stuck on \_\_\_.”
>
> [Start your first project]
>
> DJ

For account holders, replace the opening with “You made a BuildOS account a while back. I'm DJ, the founder.” Use `[Open BuildOS]` as the CTA. For verified early users, an optional honest sentence is: “The early version didn't give you enough to keep working with.” Use that only when DJ can stand behind it, not as a diagnosis of every person's departure.

**2. Personal invitation — founder help**

Subject: `A starting point for {{non_sensitive_topic}}`  
Preview: `Send the rough version; I'll help turn it into a short plan.`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> When you signed up for BuildOS, you mentioned {{verified_non_sensitive_signup_detail}}.
>
> If that's still something you're working on, reply with where it stands and the part you're stuck on. A few rough sentences are enough.
>
> I'll send back a short project outline and one suggested next step you can use in BuildOS.
>
> DJ

Omit the signup-detail sentence when there is no suitable detail. This offer requires actual founder capacity. Use only the information the recipient chooses to send. Return a usable outline promptly; provide a private BuildOS project link only through a verified, authorized workflow. Otherwise, return a pasteable plan and the appropriate product entry link. A reply is the start of assistance, not the activation metric.

**3. Day 4–5 — remove the blank page**

Subject: `What to put in BuildOS`  
Preview: `A goal, what's happened, and the thing blocking you.`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> A useful starting point doesn't need to be a finished plan.
>
> For example: “I'm launching a small website. The draft is done, but the pricing page isn't. I'd like it live next Friday. Help me identify the next three actions and what depends on what.”
>
> Swap in your own project and put that in BuildOS. Review what comes back and change anything it got wrong.
>
> [Try it with your project]
>
> DJ

This is an illustrative input, not a customer result. If using a book-specific version, substitute a chapter/revision example grounded in the recipient's stated work. For a reply-first invitation, make the final line “Reply with your version and I'll help you turn it into a starting plan,” with no product link competing beside it.

**4. Optional help after a return without useful work**

Subject: `Help getting started`  
Preview: `I can help with the first project.`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> If getting started in BuildOS felt awkward, tell me which part: getting into your account, choosing what to put in, or getting something useful back.
>
> Reply with the part that got in the way and I'll help you through it.
>
> DJ

Use only after manual review, and omit entirely if there is no meaningful reason to contact the person again. Do not mention tracking their login or email reading.

**5. After the first useful project action — the essential follow-through**

Subject: `Add what changed`  
Preview: `Give the same project one update and choose your next move.`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> Next time you work on your project, start with what changed.
>
> “I finished **_. I'm waiting on _**. The deadline moved to \_\_\_. Help me update the plan and choose the next thing to do.”
>
> Add that to the same project in BuildOS and review the changes. That's how the plan stays connected to the work you're actually doing.
>
> [Continue your project]
>
> DJ

Send only when saved project work exists and the person has not already completed this step. Where possible link directly to the authorized project, preserving the destination through login. New accounts from the waitlist should enter the existing welcome flow without receiving a duplicate second-session sequence.

After this campaign, start with one practical email every two weeks to the eligible, interested audience. Each should contain a recognizable situation, a usable prompt or method, and one contextual action. Examples: update a stale plan, identify a blocker, choose a next step, or revise work after a deadline changes. Existing articles supply ingredients; readers should get value without having to read a long article first. Review actual usage and preferences before increasing frequency.

**The destination must complete the promise**

The July deployment warning about the short-link route is no longer the immediate problem. On September 10, the start link resolved to registration with campaign parameters. The returning-user link rendered the sign-in page in a browser with the welcome-back destination preserved. Those checks do not verify login success, old-account entitlements, legacy project availability, or the complete creation/return loop.

The current server loader continues from `/welcome-back` to `/projects`, not to a particular recipient's project. Avoid labeling that generic link “Open your project” as if it were a verified direct project link. The existing short links also identify the July campaign by default; use a distinct campaign value for a new experiment without repointing old links.

Before launch, exercise one representative returning-account path and one new-account path: email destination → authentication → rough input → saved project → later update. Confirm the promised test can be performed with the account's actual access. Do not promise a trial reset, free period, or discount unless that offer is configured and authorized. Recent product-proof notes contain pending readiness checks, so the copy deliberately invites a bounded product test rather than claiming that every feature is ready for everyone.

Use the existing email tools. Account holders can use retargeting operations; contacts without a user ID need the admin draft path until they register. Stop both paths consistently when someone replies or opts out. The existing retargeting logic permits a third touch from `any_open || any_click`; that is broader than this proposal. Enforce the tighter rule by manual recipient review for the pilot. No new automation platform is necessary to test this strategy.

Check sender authentication and a real delivered message before expanding. Keep sender identity clear and opt-out easy. Google recommends SPF, DKIM, and DMARC; its requirements distinguish ordinary from bulk senders, so the cold-email article's infrastructure prescriptions should not be treated as universal Gmail rules. [Google sender guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)

**Pilot and decision rule**

Start with 5–10 eligible contacts with relevant prior context, capped by DJ's ability to fulfill every help request. If the historical six-person “left work behind” group still fits, it is a sensible first learning wave. Review replies and product friction after 72 hours before opening a 15–25-person wave. Use the refreshed list rather than assuming the historical 83 drafts remain sendable.

Use one template per invitation type initially. Tiny subject-line splits will not settle strategy. The substantive question is whether a specific project invitation produces useful work and a later update.

Track counts and denominators by segment:

- **First useful action:** recipients who create or meaningfully update saved project work within 14 days of their first send, divided by delivered recipients. Exclude automatic brief generation, background activity, and founder-created artifacts the recipient never uses.
- **Continued use:** recipients who perform meaningful project work on at least two distinct days within that same 14-day window, divided by delivered recipients. Also report this as a share of first-action users to locate the retention drop.
- **Learning:** substantive replies and the obstacle they name—timing, unclear use case, login/access, disappointing output, or another workflow that already works.
- **Delivery:** bounces, complaints, opt-outs, and confirmed delivery. Opens are diagnostic context only.

If there is a sufficiently large eligible cohort, randomly reserve around 10% within each segment before assigning waves and use the same observation window. With the historical small beta group, an 8–10-person control is only directional; a smaller qualitative pilot can reasonably proceed without a holdout. In that case, report observed post-email use, not causal lift. Handpicked warm contacts will overstate expected results for the wider list.

For a first wave of roughly ten people, use two people returning on another day as a practical signal to continue cautiously—not a benchmark or statistically reliable threshold. If people reply but never start, fix the handoff. If they start but do not return, fix the second-session value. If neither happens, revise the invitation or audience before increasing frequency. Pause expansion for complaints, repeated delivery problems, or an unfulfilled help backlog.

**Recommended next move:** refresh the first small cohort, choose the product-link or founder-help invitation for each person, verify their entry path, and review those exact messages. The campaign succeeds when someone finds a reason to keep one real project in BuildOS.
